Field guide: what goes in, what comes out

Every input table and every output number for the ten models built so far — what each field means, in plain words.

Read this first — three conventions that explain most confusion

The event is in August 2027. That is not a typo. You forecast an event before it happens. The world runs 2027-07-04 → 2027-08-03: four weeks of ordinary build-up, then arrival day, the peak bathing day, and the day after. Every model is frozen at the same instant — 2027-08-02 06:00, the morning of the peak day.

Times are 24-hour, India time (+05:30). So 06:15 is quarter past six in the morning. That matters: ritual bathing happens at dawn, so 03:00–09:00 is the busiest window of the entire month. The demo sits in the middle of it.

A timestamp repeats, and that is correct. Each table has a grain — what one row represents. D01's grain is zone × 15 min, so one timestamp has eight rows, one per zone:

2027-08-02 05:30 Z01 5,884 entries 31,285 people 3.72 p/m² 2027-08-02 05:30 Z02 3,923 entries 20,856 people 2.98 p/m² 2027-08-02 05:30 Z03 16,204 entries 15,852 people 3.30 p/m² … 8 rows, one per zone, then the clock moves on 2027-08-02 05:45 Z01 … 2,976 timestamps × 8 zones = 23,808 rows

The risk colours

Every output record can carry a colour. It is not a place — it is the severity of that one number, for that one thing, at that one moment. Each KPI is scored against a band table:

Band tablegreenamberredcriticalUsed by
utilization_pct0–80%80–90%90–100%over 100%parking, bins, bandwidth
probability_pct0–20%20–40%40–70%70–100%waterlogging, network risk
crowd_density_p_m20–2.02.0–4.04.0–5.0over 5.0crowd density
score_0_1000–4040–6060–8080–100composite risk scores
The one trick worth knowing. For some KPIs a high number is good — availability of 100% is perfect. Those are marked band_input: complement_100, so the band is applied to the shortfall instead. Verified live:
network_availability 100.00 → band sees 0.00 → green network_availability 60.00 → band sees 40.00 → red bandwidth_utilization 85.00 → band sees 85.00 → amber bandwidth_utilization 105.00 → band sees 105.00 → critical
Without that, a perfectly healthy network would have shown as critical.

Amber or worse must always carry a reason code. The contract rejects a record that turns amber without saying why — a colour with no explanation is not actionable.

The shape every output record shares

All ten models return the same record structure, which is why one dashboard parser serves all of them:

FieldWhat it means
entity_idWhich thing: a zone Z01, gate G02, car park P1, tower NT01…
entity_typeWhat kind of thing it is: zone, gate, parking_site, route, asset, facility, service_point, event
kpiWhich measure this row reports
timestampThe moment being described (not the moment you asked)
horizon_minHow far ahead of "now" that is, in minutes. 15 = quarter of an hour from now
valueThe number
unitIts unit — enforced against the registry so a model cannot drift
lower / upperThe uncertainty band. quantile_level: 0.8 means the truth should land inside it about 80% of the time
risk_levelgreen / amber / red / critical, from the tables above
reason_codesWhy it is coloured. Mandatory from amber upwards
recommendationA proposed action — always with requires_approval: true
detailsModel-specific working: which backend ran, intermediate figures, the capped view of a number

M21 — Weather Impact

What is the weather, and how does it change risk everywhere else?

The only model that depends on nothing else. Built first because nine others read it. Pure formula — no learning, no training, no randomness.

INPUT

D12 weather_hourly — 744 rows, one per hour

FieldMeansReal value at 06:00
temperature_cair temperature, °C22.4
humidity_pctrelative humidity95.0
rain_mmrainfall that hour, mm0.0
wind_kmhwind speed — disperses pollution and heat13.6
heat_index_cwhat it feels like once humidity is counted23.2
visibility_mvisibility, may be empty—

This data is real. Fetched from Open-Meteo for this location, then shifted onto the 2027 event dates. So: real weather patterns, on the event's calendar.

Also reads D03 zones for each zone's drainage capacity and whether it sits low — which is what decides where water pools.

OUTPUT 182 records · 9 entities (the venue + 8 zones) · every 15 min for 3 hours
KPIUnitWhat it tells you
temperature°CPlain air temperature.
heat_index°CWhat it feels like. In humid air this runs well above the thermometer, and it is what drives heat casualties — not the raw temperature.
rainfall_intensitymm/hrHow hard it is raining.
waterlogging_probability%Per zone. Chance of standing water, from rainfall against that zone's own drainage, plus a penalty if it is low-lying. A slip hazard in a dense crowd.
weather_arrival_multiplierratioHow much the weather suppresses attendance. 0.85 means 15% fewer people turn up.
weather_medical_multiplierratioHow much it raises medical cases. Heat makes people faint.
weather_traffic_speed_multiplierratioHow much it slows traffic.

Those three multipliers are the whole point of the model: other models read them. M21 does not just report weather, it translates weather into the language each other model speaks.

M01 — Footfall Forecast

How many people will arrive, by zone and gate, over the next three hours?

The keystone. Nine of the other built models read it. If M01 is wrong, most of the twin is wrong.

INPUT

D01 footfall_15min — 23,808 rows · zone × 15 min

FieldMeans
zone_idWhich area: Z01–Z08 (the ghats, corridors, market, transit hub)
entriesPeople who walked into this zone during those 15 minutes
exitsPeople who walked out
populationPeople standing there at the end of the step — the running total
density_p_m2People per square metre. The safety number. Above 4 is dangerous; above 5 is a crush risk
gate_idSet only on gate rows

Plus gate_entries (queue length and capacity at each of the four gates), D12 weather, D21 event_calendar and D03 zones. Upstream: M21.

OUTPUT 156 records · 12 entities (8 zones + 4 gates) · every 15 min for 3 hours
KPIUnitWhat it tells you
expected_footfallpersons/hrThe arrival rate at each zone and gate, with an 80% band. This is the number everything downstream is built on.
peak_footfallpersons/hrThe single busiest moment in the window, and details.peak_at says when. That timing is the operational value: it tells you how long you have.
Z01 expected_footfall = 23,767 persons/hr band 23,458 – 25,161

M05 — Parking Demand

How full will each car park get, and when does it overflow?

INPUT

D06 parking_15min — site × 15 min

FieldMeansAt 06:00
parking_idP1 North lot, P2 Main lot, P3 Market lot—
entriesCars arriving — note this is demand, not cars admitted1,495
exitsCars leaving1,307
occupiedBays in use — capped at capacity7,300
capacityTotal bays at that site7,300

Plus D01 footfall and D21 calendar. Upstream: M01.

OUTPUT 108 records · 3 car parks
KPIUnitWhat it tells you
parking_demandvehiclesHow many cars want a bay. Can exceed the number of bays.
parking_occupancy%Demand ÷ capacity. Can go above 100%. 123% means 23% of arriving drivers have nowhere to go.
parking_search_timeminHow long to find a space. Flat until the lot is 80% full, then climbs steeply — which is how parking actually behaves.
Why occupancy exceeds 100%. The stored occupied column is capped at capacity, so a model trained on it could never predict an overflow — the one thing this model exists to warn about. So M05 forecasts demand instead. details.occupied_spaces still carries the capped, physically-parked count.

M06 — Transit / Shuttle Demand

How many passengers and how many shuttle vehicles does each route need?

The first model with two upstreams — M01 and M05.

INPUT

FieldMeansAt 06:00

boardingsPeople getting on13,968

OUTPUT 72 records · 2 routes
KPIUnitWhat it tells you
shuttle_demandpersons/hrPassengers wanting to travel on that route.

M15 — Water Demand

How much water does each zone need, is there a shortfall, and how many tankers?

INPUT

D14 water_15min — 23,808 rows · zone × 15 min

FieldMeans
consumption_lLitres drunk and used in that zone in 15 minutes
supply_lLitres the pumps delivered to it
storage_lLitres sitting in tanks
asset_idThe pump serving that zone (PMP01–03), empty where there is none

Plus D01 footfall, D12 weather, D21 calendar. Upstream: M01 and M21 — 48,400 input rows in total.

OUTPUT 296 records · 8 zones
KPIUnitWhat it tells you
expected_water_demandL/hrLitres per hour that zone will want.
water_supply_demand_gapL/hrPositive means a shortage. Negative is spare capacity — reported rather than hidden, because "how much slack have we got" is a real question.
drinking_water_point_requirementpointsTaps needed, sized once against the busiest moment in the window — you place a water point for the peak, not the average.
water_tanker_requirementvehiclesTankers to cover the shortfall for four hours.

Under a power failure a pump loses output, the gap widens, and tankers get called. Leakage is not modelled — a real network loses a lot between pump and tap, so the true gap would be worse.

M17 — Food & Supply

How much food is needed, how long does stock last, how many deliveries?

INPUT

D17 food_inventory_hourly — outlet × hour

FieldMeans

meals_soldMeals sold that hour
stock_mealsMeals left on the shelf
deliveriesMeals that arrived that hour
lead_time_hHours between ordering and arrival. The number the whole model turns on
OUTPUT 72 records · 2 outlets · hourly for 12 hours
KPIUnitWhat it tells you
food_demandmeals/hrMeals people will want.
food_stock_coveragehoursHow long the shelf lasts at that rate. The decision number — if it is shorter than the delivery lead time, ordering now is already too late.
delivery_requirementtrips/dayVehicle trips needed, at 2,000 meals per load.
Two things differ here. The data is hourly, not 15-minute. And the horizon is 12 hours, deliberately longer than the 6-hour lead time — inside a shorter window nothing you order can arrive, so a supply disruption would show no effect at all.

It also publishes details.load_utilization specifically for M12 (fire risk) to read later — overloaded electrics start fires.

M20 — Network Capacity

Will communications — CCTV backhaul and command links — overload or fail?

Everything in the control room depends on this. If the backhaul dies, the operators lose the cameras, the dashboards and each other.

INPUT

D19 network_5min — tower × 5 min

FieldMeansAt 06:00
tower_idNT01, NT02, NT03—
bandwidth_used_mbpsTraffic flowing — camera streams plus the crowd's phones391.7
capacity_mbpsWhat the link can carry2,000.0
latency_msRound-trip delay12.0
packet_loss_pctShare of data lost0.0
upIs the tower alive?true

This is the finest-grained table in the project — every 5 minutes, because a network problem develops in seconds, not quarter-hours.

OUTPUT 324 records · 3 towers · every 5 min for 3 hours
KPIUnitWhat it tells you
bandwidth_utilization%How much of the link is in use. 19.81% of a 2,000 Mbps link is 396 Mbps.
network_availability%Share of time the tower is up. Higher is better — the colour is applied to the shortfall.
network_capacity_risk%Chance of running out of headroom. An S-curve that is near zero while quiet and climbs sharply past 70% usage — so it warns before saturation rather than reporting it after.
Being straight about this one: in this world the network never runs out. Peak usage is 27.5%, so it is green all day and network_capacity_risk sits near zero. That is the correct answer for these tower capacities — the threshold was left where an operator would want it rather than lowered to make the demo livelier. The genuinely useful KPI here is availability, which drops when a substation fails and takes NT02 down with it.

The AQI breakpoints are transcription placeholders that need checking against the official CPCB table; all three CO₂ emission factors are placeholders; and the CO₂ traffic term is zero because M04 does not exist — every record says so in details.traffic_term.
